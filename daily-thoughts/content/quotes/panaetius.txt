# Cicero, On Duties 1.110
q: Each person must hold firmly to their own nature,
q: as long as it is not wicked, so that they keep the fitting character that is proper to them.
c: Cicero bases this passage on Panaetius' theory of the four roles we play: our shared human nature, our individual nature, our circumstances and our chosen path. We should not fight our own talents and temperament to imitate someone else. Act well as the particular person you are.
p: Do one thing today that plays to your real strengths, instead of imitating someone else's.
s: Based on Panaetius' On Appropriate Action, as adapted by Cicero, On Duties 1.107–117.

# Aulus Gellius, Attic Nights 13.28
q: The life of people who live among affairs and want to be useful to themselves and others
q: is like the stance of a pancratiast: always on guard, alert to the blows that come from every side.
c: Gellius quotes this from Panaetius' book On Appropriate Action. The pankration was a brutal Greek combat sport mixing boxing and wrestling. A person active in public life, Panaetius says, must be just as watchful, ready to block and respond to dangers coming from all directions.
e: c. 140 BC · Egypt and the East | The embassy with Scipio | Panaetius travelled with his friend Scipio Aemilianus, the destroyer of Carthage, on a Roman embassy to Egypt, Syria and the eastern kingdoms. It was a journey through a world of courts, intrigue and power, the kind of life where his advice about staying on guard was not theory.
p: Before an important conversation today, take a moment to prepare: where could it go wrong, and how will you respond?

# Cicero, On Duties 1.90
q: Just as horses that have become wild from fighting are handed to trainers,
q: so people who have become unruly through success should be led into the training ground of reason.
c: Cicero tells us that Panaetius used to report this saying of his friend Scipio Africanus. Success can make people arrogant and careless. Like a horse made skittish by battle, they need to be retrained, through reflection, to stay calm and humble.
p: After any success today, take a moment to come back down and look at it soberly.
s: Saying of Scipio reported by Panaetius, via Cicero, On Duties 1.90.
