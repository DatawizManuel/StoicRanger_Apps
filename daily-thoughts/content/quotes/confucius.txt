# Analects 1.1
q: Is it not a pleasure to learn something and practise it at the right time?
q: Is it not a joy to have friends come from far away? Is it not noble to remain unbothered when others do not recognise you?
c: These are the first words of the Analects, the collection of Confucius' sayings put together by his disciples. They set out his whole programme in three lines: steady learning put into practice, friendship based on shared values, and a quiet independence from the need for recognition.
p: Learn one thing today and use it before the day ends.

# Analects 2.4
q: At fifteen I set my heart on learning. At thirty I stood firm. At forty I had no doubts. At fifty I knew the will of Heaven.
q: At sixty my ear was attuned. At seventy I could follow my heart's desire without overstepping what is right.
c: Confucius sums up his life in a few lines. The final stage is the most striking: after a lifetime of training, what he wants and what is right have become the same. For the Stoics too, the goal was a character so formed that doing right feels natural.
p: Ask yourself where you are on your own path. What is the next stage you are working toward?

# Analects 2.17
q: Shall I teach you what knowledge is?
q: When you know a thing, to recognise that you know it; when you do not know it, to recognise that you do not know it. That is knowledge.
c: Confucius says this to his disciple Zilu, who was brave but rash and inclined to claim more than he knew. The lesson is the same as Socrates': true knowledge begins with honesty about the limits of what we know.
p: Say "I don't know" at least once today when it's true.

# Analects 15.24
q: Is there one word that can guide a person throughout life?
q: "Reciprocity", perhaps: what you do not want for yourself, do not do to others.
c: Asked by his disciple Zigong for a single principle to live by, Confucius gave this answer. It is one of the earliest forms of what is now called the Golden Rule, stated five centuries before Christ.
p: Before acting toward someone today, ask how you would feel on the receiving end.

# Analects 4.17
q: When you see a person of worth, think of how to become their equal.
q: When you see someone unworthy, turn inward and examine yourself.
c: Confucius turns every encounter into a lesson. Good people show us what to aim for. Bad examples are not a reason to feel superior, but a mirror: do I have this fault too?
p: When someone irritates you today, ask whether you share any part of the fault you see.

# Analects 7.22
q: When three people walk together, one of them is sure to be my teacher.
q: I pick out their good qualities and follow them, and their bad qualities and correct them in myself.
c: Confucius believed there is something to learn from everyone. The good show us what to imitate; the bad show us what to avoid. No encounter is wasted for someone who is willing to learn.
p: In each conversation today, look for one thing to learn from the other person.

# Analects 9.28
q: Only when the year grows cold
q: do we see that the pine and the cypress are the last to lose their leaves.
c: In good times, everyone looks strong. It is only in hardship that we see who truly has character, just as evergreens stand out only in winter. Difficulty reveals what easy times hide.
e: c. 489 BC · Between Chen and Cai | Hunger on the road | During his years of wandering, Confucius and his disciples were trapped between the states of Chen and Cai, surrounded by hostile forces. They ran out of food, and many fell ill. Confucius went on teaching, reciting and playing his lute. When the angry Zilu asked if a gentleman ever had to suffer such want, Confucius answered that a gentleman holds firm in hardship.
p: When something hard happens today, see it as the cold that shows your evergreen qualities.

# Analects 15.2
q: The exemplary person holds firm in hardship.
q: The petty person in hardship loses all restraint.
c: Confucius gave this answer when his disciple Zilu, angry and hungry, asked whether a gentleman should ever be reduced to such need. Hardship comes to all. What differs is how we behave in it.
p: When you feel pressed today, hold to your standards a little more firmly.

# Analects 4.14
q: Do not worry about having no position; worry about whether you are fit to hold one.
q: Do not worry that no one knows you; seek to be worth knowing.
c: Confucius spent much of his life without the office he wanted. Instead of complaining, he focused on what was in his control: becoming capable. Recognition is not up to us; worthiness is.
p: Spend effort today on becoming more capable, not on being noticed.

# Analects 15.21
q: The exemplary person makes demands on themselves.
q: The petty person makes demands on others.
c: The junzi, the exemplary person, looks inward first when something goes wrong. The petty person blames others. It is the same distinction Epictetus made between the educated and the uneducated.
p: When something goes wrong today, ask first what you could have done differently.

# Analects 2.15
q: Learning without thinking is useless.
q: Thinking without learning is dangerous.
c: Confucius balances study and reflection. Reading and memorising without thinking leaves us confused. Thinking without studying what others have learned leaves us in danger of foolish mistakes.
p: Today, take one thing you've read recently and spend ten minutes thinking about it.

# Analects 9.17
q: Standing by a river, the Master said:
q: "It passes on like this, never ceasing, day or night."
c: This short scene has been interpreted in many ways: as a lament for passing time, as a symbol of tireless effort, as a reminder of constant change. Like Heraclitus at almost the same time on the other side of the world, Confucius saw in a river the image of life itself.
p: Watch something that keeps moving today: water, traffic, clouds. Let it remind you that today, too, is passing.

# Analects 7.16
q: Eating coarse rice, drinking water, using my bent arm for a pillow, there is still joy in these.
q: Wealth and honours gained unjustly are to me like floating clouds.
c: Confucius describes a simple life without regret. Joy does not depend on luxury; it depends on living rightly. Wealth gained wrongly is as unreal and passing as a cloud.
p: Find joy in one simple thing today, and let go of one thing you want that isn't worth its price.

# Analects 6.11
q: How worthy was Hui! A single bowl of rice, a ladle of water, living in a poor alley.
q: Others could not have borne the hardship, but Hui never let it spoil his joy.
c: Yan Hui was Confucius' favourite disciple, poor all his life but devoted to learning and virtue. Confucius praises him for finding contentment in the simplest conditions. It is the same freedom that Epicurus and Seneca praised.
e: c. 481 BC · Lu | The death of Yan Hui | Yan Hui died young, around the age of forty. Confucius wept bitterly and cried out: "Alas! Heaven has destroyed me! Heaven has destroyed me!" When disciples said he was grieving excessively, he answered: "If I am not to grieve for this man, for whom should I grieve?"
p: Be content today with what you have, as Hui was.

# Analects 12.1
q: To master oneself and return to propriety is humaneness.
q: If for one day you master yourself and return to propriety, the whole world will turn to humaneness.
c: Asked by Yan Hui about ren, humaneness or goodness, Confucius gave this answer. Goodness starts with self-discipline. And it comes from oneself, not from others. When Hui asked for specifics, Confucius said: look at nothing, listen to nothing, say nothing and do nothing contrary to propriety.
p: Master yourself in one small moment today, and act with care toward others.

# Analects 15.30
q: To make a mistake and not correct it:
q: that is what is truly called a mistake.
c: Everyone makes mistakes. Confucius does not condemn them; he condemns not correcting them. Errors are part of learning. Refusing to admit and fix them is the real failure.
p: Correct one mistake today instead of defending it.

# Analects 1.8
q: Hold faithfulness and sincerity as first principles.
q: When you have faults, do not be afraid to abandon them.
c: Confucius tells his disciples that they will not be respected if they are not serious, and that their learning will not be solid. Sincerity, loyalty and a willingness to change are the foundations of character.
p: Let go of one fault today without being afraid of what it says about you.

# Analects 4.16
q: The exemplary person understands what is right.
q: The petty person understands what is profitable.
c: Confucius often contrasts the junzi, the exemplary person, with the xiaoren, the petty person. The difference is in what they look at. One asks: is it right? The other asks: what do I gain?
p: In one choice today, ask "is it right?" before asking "what's in it for me?"

# Analects 2.1
q: One who governs through virtue
q: is like the North Star, which stays in its place while all the other stars pay homage to it.
c: Confucius believed that leaders should rule by moral example rather than by force or punishment. A virtuous leader stays steady, and others naturally orient themselves around them.
e: c. 500 BC · Jiagu | The meeting at Jiagu | According to tradition, Confucius accompanied the Duke of Lu to a peace meeting with the more powerful state of Qi. When Qi tried to intimidate the duke with armed dancers, Confucius insisted on proper ritual and rebuked the Qi ruler. Ashamed, Qi returned lands it had taken from Lu.
p: Lead by example today in one area where others watch you.
s: Jiagu: Zuo Zhuan, Duke Ding 10; Sima Qian, Records of the Grand Historian 47.

# Analects 12.17
q: To govern is to set things right.
q: If you lead by being upright, who will dare not to be upright?
c: Asked by a powerful minister of Lu how to govern, Confucius played on the similarity of two Chinese words: zheng, "to govern", and zheng, "to be upright". Government begins with the ruler's own conduct.
p: Before asking anyone else to do the right thing today, do it yourself.

# Analects 13.3
q: If names are not correct, language is not in accord with the truth of things.
q: If language is not in accord with the truth of things, affairs cannot be carried on to success.
c: Asked what he would do first if he governed, Confucius surprised his disciples: he would rectify the names. When words no longer match reality, when a ruler is not acting as a ruler or a father as a father, confusion spreads through everything.
p: Call one thing by its honest name today, even if it's uncomfortable.

# Analects 7.8
q: I do not open the way for those who are not eager to learn, nor help out those who are not anxious to explain themselves.
q: If I show one corner and they cannot come back with the other three, I do not repeat the lesson.
c: Confucius expected his students to think for themselves. A teacher can point the way, but the student has to walk it. Learning that is spoon-fed does not last.
p: When you learn something today, try to work out the "other three corners" yourself.

# Analects 14.27
q: The exemplary person is ashamed
q: when their words exceed their deeds.
c: Confucius distrusted fine speech. Talking is easy; acting is hard. He wanted his students' actions to go beyond their words, not the other way round.
p: Today, do more than you say.

# Analects 17.2
q: By nature people are close to one another.
q: Through practice they grow far apart.
c: Confucius believed people are born much alike. What separates the good from the bad is not birth but habit and training. Like Aristotle and the Stoics, he saw character as something built through daily practice.
p: Practise one good habit today, knowing it is shaping who you become.

# Analects 9.26
q: The commander of three armies can be taken from them,
q: but the will of even a common person cannot be taken from them.
c: An army can lose its general, but no one can take away a person's resolve unless they give it up themselves. It is one of the most Stoic sentences in the Analects: our will is our own.
e: c. 496 BC · Kuang | Mistaken for an enemy | While travelling, Confucius was surrounded at Kuang by people who mistook him for Yang Hu, a hated official who had once oppressed them. He and his disciples were held for days. Confucius stayed calm, saying that if Heaven meant this culture to survive, the people of Kuang could do nothing to him.
p: Decide one thing today that no pressure will make you give up.
s: Kuang: Analects 9.5.
