# Seneca, On Anger 3.38
q: I am not angry.
q: But I am not sure that I ought not to be.
c: Seneca tells how Diogenes, while lecturing on anger, was spat on in the face by an insolent young man. Diogenes wiped his face, stayed calm and gave this mild, ironic answer. He refused to let the insult take over, and still made his point.
e: 155 BC · Rome | The philosophers' embassy | Athens sent three philosophers to the Roman Senate to appeal a heavy fine: Carneades of the Academy, Critolaus the Peripatetic and Diogenes for the Stoics. While they waited, they lectured to crowds of young Romans. Carneades argued for justice one day and against it the next, and Cato the Elder urged the Senate to send them home quickly. Ancient writers praised Diogenes for his sober, restrained manner.
p: If someone provokes you today, answer calmly, and if needed, with a little humour.
s: Embassy: Cicero, On the Orator 2.155; Plutarch, Cato the Elder 22.

# Cicero, On Duties 3.51
q: I have brought my grain here; I have offered it for sale; I sell it no dearer than others.
q: Where is the wrong in that, if I tell no lie?
c: This is how Cicero has Diogenes answer the case of the grain merchant who arrives in Rhodes during a famine, knowing more ships are on the way. Diogenes argues that honesty forbids lying, but does not require you to volunteer every fact that would lower your price. His student Antipater disagreed. The debate is still discussed in business ethics.
p: Ask yourself in one deal or conversation today: am I just avoiding lies, or am I being truly open?
s: Speech as reconstructed by Cicero, On Duties 3.50–53.

# Diogenes Laertius, Lives 7.88
q: The goal of life is to reason well
q: in the selection and rejection of things according to nature.
c: Diogenes gave this formulation of the Stoic goal. We cannot control outcomes, but we can choose well among the options life presents: health over sickness, safety over danger, where appropriate. The good lies in the quality of the choosing.
p: For one choice today, focus on choosing well rather than on guaranteeing the outcome.
s: Formula reported by Diogenes Laertius 7.88 and Stobaeus 2.7.6.
