## An aristocrat of Ephesus
Heraclitus lived around 535 to 475 BC in Ephesus, a rich Greek city on the coast of Ionia in modern Turkey. He came from an old aristocratic family that held the hereditary title of "king", a priestly office. According to Diogenes Laertius, Heraclitus gave the title up in favour of his brother.

He was proud, solitary and contemptuous of nearly everyone. He mocked Homer, Hesiod and Pythagoras, and said that learning many things does not teach understanding. When the people of Ephesus exiled his friend Hermodorus, Heraclitus declared that all the adult Ephesians should hang themselves and leave the city to the boys. One story says he withdrew from public life to play dice with children at the temple of Artemis, telling the citizens that this was better than taking part in their politics.

## The obscure one
Heraclitus wrote a single book, which he is said to have deposited in the great temple of Artemis at Ephesus. It is lost, but around 130 short fragments survive, quoted by later writers. They are written in compressed, riddling sentences, which earned him the nickname "the Obscure". Socrates is said to have remarked that what he understood of the book was excellent, and what he did not understand was probably excellent too, but it would take a diver from Delos to get to the bottom of it.

## Logos, fire and change
Heraclitus taught that there is a single law or reason, the logos, which governs everything, though most people do not notice it, living as if asleep. The world is like an ever-living fire, kindling and going out in measures. Everything is in constant change, and the world holds together through the tension of opposites, like a bow or a lyre. "The road up and the road down are one and the same." Plato summed up his view with the famous image that you cannot step into the same river twice, because new waters are always flowing.

He also wrote that "character is a person's fate", a line Stoics would have agreed with.

## His influence on the Stoics
The Stoics took more from Heraclitus than from almost any other early thinker. Zeno and Cleanthes adopted his ideas of a rational logos running through the universe and of fire as its basic element. Cleanthes wrote a commentary on his book. Marcus Aurelius quotes him several times in the Meditations, including the warning not to act and speak like people asleep.

## Death and image
Diogenes Laertius tells that Heraclitus fell ill with dropsy, buried himself in cow dung in the hope that its warmth would draw out the fluid, and died. The story is probably invented, like many ancient philosophers' deaths.

Later tradition paired him with Democritus as "the weeping philosopher", who mourned the folly of the world, while Democritus laughed at it. The portrait on his coin comes from a painting of this weeping Heraclitus by the Dutch artist Johannes Moreelse, made around 1630. No portrait made in his lifetime survives.
