## From the Tigris to Athens
Diogenes was born around 230 BC in Seleucia on the Tigris, the great Greek city founded near ancient Babylon, which is why he was called "the Babylonian". He came to Athens, studied with Chrysippus and then with Zeno of Tarsus, and succeeded Zeno as head of the Stoa. He led the school for several decades and lived to over eighty.

## Language, music and the mind
Diogenes wrote on a wide range of subjects. His handbook On Voice set out the Stoic theory of language: sounds, letters, parts of speech and the difference between a mere noise and a meaningful word. It shaped Greek and Roman grammar for centuries. He also wrote On Music, arguing that music has real effects on character, a claim the Epicurean Philodemus later attacked point by point in a book whose remains were found at Herculaneum. In ethics he refined the Stoic idea of value, explaining how things that are not good in themselves, such as health or money, can still be rationally chosen.

## The philosophers' embassy of 155 BC
Diogenes is best remembered for a journey to Rome. In 155 BC Athens was fined 500 talents for raiding the town of Oropus. To appeal, the Athenians sent three of their leading philosophers as ambassadors: Carneades of the Academy, Critolaus of the Peripatetic school and Diogenes for the Stoics.

While they waited for the Senate, the three men gave public lectures, and young Romans flocked to hear them. Carneades caused a scandal by speaking in praise of justice one day and arguing against it the next. Cato the Elder, alarmed that Greek cleverness would corrupt Roman youth, urged the Senate to settle the matter quickly and send the philosophers home. The fine was reduced to 100 talents. Ancient writers praised Diogenes in particular for his sober and restrained style of speaking. For many Romans, this was their first direct encounter with Greek philosophy, and with Stoicism in particular.

## The grain merchant of Rhodes
Cicero preserves a famous debate between Diogenes and his student Antipater. A merchant brings a cargo of grain to Rhodes during a famine. He knows that several other grain ships are on their way and will arrive soon. Must he tell the Rhodians, which will lower his price, or may he keep quiet and sell at the famine price?

Diogenes argued that the merchant must not lie, but he is not obliged to volunteer every piece of information that would help the buyer. Antipater argued that honesty requires full disclosure, because we are all members of one human community. The case is still discussed in business ethics today.

## His students
Diogenes taught many of the people who would carry Stoicism forward: Antipater of Tarsus, who succeeded him; Panaetius of Rhodes, who brought the school to the Roman elite; and Boethus of Sidon. He died around 150 to 140 BC.

## Why there is no portrait
No reliable portrait of Diogenes of Babylon exists, so his coin carries the gold Stoa column. Only fragments of his writings survive, quoted by Cicero, Philodemus and others, so genuine quotations are scarce.
