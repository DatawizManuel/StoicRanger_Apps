## A poor boy in Lu
Confucius, in Chinese Kongzi or Kong Fuzi, "Master Kong", was born in 551 BC near Qufu in the small state of Lu, in today's Shandong province. His personal name was Kong Qiu. His father, Shuliang He, was an old soldier who died when Confucius was about three, and the boy grew up in poverty with his mother, Yan Zhengzai.

He later said that because he was poor and of humble station in his youth, he became skilled in many menial things. As a young man he worked as a keeper of granaries and of livestock for a noble family. He married at nineteen and had a son and a daughter. Meanwhile he taught himself the rites, music, history and poetry of the early Zhou dynasty, which he regarded as a golden age of good order.

## In government
Confucius began to take students and became known for his learning. According to tradition, around the age of fifty he entered the service of the Duke of Lu and rose to high office, possibly as Minister of Crime. At a diplomatic meeting with the more powerful state of Qi at Jiagu in 500 BC, his insistence on correct ritual is said to have shamed Qi into returning territory it had taken from Lu.

His career did not last. Traditional accounts say that Qi, worried by Lu's growing strength, sent the duke a gift of dancing girls and fine horses, and the duke neglected government to enjoy them. Around 497 BC Confucius left Lu in disappointment.

## Fourteen years of wandering
For the next fourteen years Confucius travelled from state to state with a small band of disciples, looking for a ruler who would put his ideas into practice. None did. He was threatened, mistaken for an enemy and at one point nearly starved when he and his followers were trapped between the states of Chen and Cai. When a disciple asked whether a gentleman ever finds himself in such need, Confucius answered that a gentleman holds firm in hardship, while a petty person in hardship loses all restraint.

## Teacher of the ages
Around 484 BC, aged nearly seventy, Confucius returned to Lu. He spent his last years teaching and, according to tradition, editing the classic books of poetry, history and ritual. He is said to have had three thousand students, of whom seventy-two mastered his teaching. He outlived his son and his favourite disciple, Yan Hui, whose death left him crying out that heaven had destroyed him. Confucius died in 479 BC.

## The Analects
Confucius wrote no book of his own ideas. His sayings and conversations were collected by his disciples and their students in the Analects. They describe the ideal of the junzi, the exemplary person, who cultivates ren, humaneness or goodness, and li, ritual propriety, and who leads by moral example rather than force. Asked for a single word to guide one's whole life, he offered "reciprocity" and explained: "Do not impose on others what you do not wish for yourself."

He summed up his own life in a famous passage: at fifteen he set his heart on learning, at thirty he stood firm, at forty he had no doubts, at fifty he knew the will of heaven, at sixty his ear was attuned, and at seventy he could follow his heart's desire without overstepping what was right.

## Legacy
Under the Han dynasty in the second century BC, his teachings became the official philosophy of the Chinese state, and they shaped education and government in China, Korea, Japan and Vietnam for two thousand years. His stress on self-cultivation, duty and steady character has much in common with Stoic ethics. The image on his coin is based on a portrait attributed to the Tang painter Wu Daozi.
