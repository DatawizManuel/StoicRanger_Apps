## A Roman knight who taught in Greek
Gaius Musonius Rufus was born before AD 30 in Volsinii, an old Etruscan town in central Italy, today Bolsena. He belonged to the equestrian order, the class of Roman knights just below the senators. He taught philosophy in Rome, in Greek, and became so respected that later writers called him "the Roman Socrates".

## Exile under Nero
In AD 65, after the Pisonian conspiracy against Nero, Musonius was exiled to Gyara, a barren, waterless island in the Aegean used as a prison for political enemies. People still sailed out to visit him and hear him teach. A later story says he discovered a spring on the island, making it more livable. He was recalled after Nero's death in 68.

In 69, the year of the four emperors, armies were marching on Rome. Musonius joined a delegation of senators sent to meet the troops of Vespasian's general outside the city. He walked among the soldiers lecturing on the blessings of peace and the dangers of war. Tacitus says the soldiers mocked him and he barely escaped being beaten.

In 70 he prosecuted Publius Egnatius Celer, a Stoic who had given false testimony that led to the death of the senator Barea Soranus, and won. When Vespasian expelled philosophers from Rome in 71, Musonius was at first allowed to stay, but he was later exiled again and returned only under the emperor Titus.

## Practice above theory
Musonius wrote nothing himself. His teaching survives through notes taken by a student named Lucius, preserved as some twenty-one short lectures, along with sayings quoted by later writers. They are practical and direct. Philosophy, he said, is useless unless it changes how you live, just as medicine is useless unless it heals. Virtue is not only known but trained, through hardship, simple food, cold and heat, and steady practice.

## Women, marriage and family
Musonius held views that were unusual in Rome. He argued that women have the same capacity for reason and virtue as men and should study philosophy. Daughters should receive the same education as sons. Marriage should be a full partnership of two people who share everything and care for each other. He taught that men as well as women should limit sex to marriage, opposed the common practice of abandoning unwanted newborns, and praised large families.

## Simple living
He recommended plain food, mostly vegetables, grains, fruit and cheese, prepared simply. He thought clothes and houses should protect the body, not display wealth, and that farming was a fitting occupation for a philosopher, because it kept body and mind healthy and left time to teach.

## His students
His most famous student was Epictetus, who heard him while still a slave and quotes him with deep respect in the Discourses. Another was the orator Dio Chrysostom. Through Epictetus, Musonius' style of practical, demanding Stoicism reached Marcus Aurelius.

No reliable portrait of Musonius is known, so his coin carries the gold Stoa column. The lecture notes of Lucius do give us his ideas in some detail, so short quotations from them can be used for one-pagers.
