## A doctor's son from Stagira
Aristotle was born in 384 BC in Stagira, a small Greek town in the Chalcidice peninsula of northern Greece. His father Nicomachus was the court physician of King Amyntas III of Macedon, the grandfather of Alexander the Great. Both parents died when he was young, and he was raised by a guardian.

## Twenty years in the Academy
At seventeen Aristotle went to Athens and joined Plato's Academy. He stayed for twenty years, first as a student and then as a teacher, until Plato's death in 347 BC. Plato is said to have called him "the mind of the school". Aristotle admired his teacher, but came to reject the theory of Forms; in a famous line, he said that Plato was dear to him, but truth was dearer.

## Travels and Alexander
After leaving Athens, Aristotle spent several years in Assos, at the court of the ruler Hermias, whose niece Pythias he married, and then on the island of Lesbos. There, with his friend Theophrastus, he made close studies of marine animals, careful observations that made him the founder of biology as a science.

In 343 BC King Philip II of Macedon invited him to tutor his thirteen-year-old son Alexander. Aristotle taught him for several years at Mieza, a quiet sanctuary of the Nymphs. How much of Aristotle's thinking rubbed off on the future conqueror is debated, but Alexander is said to have slept with a copy of the Iliad annotated by his teacher.

## The Lyceum
In 335 BC Aristotle returned to Athens and founded his own school in the Lyceum, a gymnasium sacred to Apollo Lyceius. He liked to teach while walking along its covered walkways, and his followers became known as the Peripatetics, "those who walk around". The school collected books, gathered information on the constitutions of 158 Greek cities and organised research in almost every field.

## Writings
The works that survive are mostly his lecture notes, which is why they can be dense and dry. They cover an astonishing range: logic in the Organon, nature in the Physics, first principles in the Metaphysics, the soul in On the Soul, animals, politics, rhetoric and poetry. His Nicomachean Ethics argues that the goal of life is eudaimonia, flourishing, achieved through virtues of character built up by habit, each of them a mean between two extremes, such as courage between cowardice and recklessness.

Here the Stoics parted ways with him. Aristotle held that a good life also needs some external goods, such as health, friends and a measure of wealth. The Stoics insisted that virtue alone is enough.

## Flight and death
When Alexander died in 323 BC, anti-Macedonian feeling exploded in Athens and Aristotle was charged with impiety. Remembering Socrates, he left the city, saying he would not let the Athenians sin twice against philosophy. He died the following year, in 322 BC, at Chalcis on the island of Euboea, aged about sixty-two.

## Legacy
His works were edited in the first century BC by Andronicus of Rhodes, preserved by Arab and Byzantine scholars and rediscovered in Western Europe in the twelfth and thirteenth centuries. Medieval writers simply called him "the Philosopher". For nearly two thousand years, logic, natural science and ethics were studied in the framework he built.
