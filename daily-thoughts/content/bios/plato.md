## A young aristocrat
Plato was born around 428 BC in Athens into one of the city's oldest aristocratic families. Ancient tradition says his real name was Aristocles and that "Plato", meaning "broad", was a nickname, perhaps for his shoulders, his forehead or his style. As a young man he may have wrestled and written poetry. Two of his relatives, Critias and Charmides, were among the Thirty, the oligarchs who ruled Athens by terror in 404 BC. Their violence turned him away from a political career.

## Socrates and his death
Plato became one of the young men around Socrates. Socrates' execution in 399 BC, when Plato was about twenty-eight, was the turning point of his life. It convinced him that no city could be just until philosophers became rulers or rulers became philosophers. Almost all of his writings give Socrates the leading role.

## Travels and Sicily
After Socrates' death Plato left Athens and travelled, to Megara, perhaps to Egypt, and to southern Italy, where he met Pythagorean thinkers such as Archytas of Tarentum. Around 388 BC he visited the court of Dionysius I, tyrant of Syracuse. According to a later story, the visit ended badly and Plato was sold into slavery on the island of Aegina, until a man named Anniceris bought his freedom.

Later in life he went to Syracuse twice more, in 367 and 361 BC, at the urging of his friend Dion, hoping to turn the young ruler Dionysius II into a philosopher-king. Both attempts failed, and Plato was lucky to get home.

## The Academy
Around 387 BC Plato founded a school in a grove sacred to the hero Akademos, just outside the walls of Athens. The Academy taught mathematics, astronomy, politics and philosophy, and it lasted in some form until Sulla's army sacked Athens in 86 BC. Aristotle studied there for twenty years. Zeno, the founder of Stoicism, attended lectures there under Polemo.

## The dialogues
Plato wrote philosophy as drama: conversations in which characters argue their way toward the truth. All of his roughly thirty-five dialogues have survived, among them the Apology, Crito and Phaedo on Socrates' trial and death, the Symposium on love, the Republic on justice and the ideal city, the Timaeus on the creation of the world, and the Laws, his last and longest work.

His central ideas include the theory of Forms, the belief that behind the changing things we see stand perfect, unchanging realities such as Justice and Beauty; the division of the soul into reason, spirit and appetite; and the four cardinal virtues of wisdom, courage, moderation and justice. The Stoics took over these four virtues as the core of their ethics. His allegory of the cave, in which prisoners mistake shadows for reality, is one of the most famous images in philosophy.

## Death and legacy
Plato died around 348 BC at about eighty and was buried in the Academy. The philosopher Alfred North Whitehead later wrote that the whole European philosophical tradition is a series of footnotes to Plato. The Stoics disagreed with him on many points, but they argued in a world he had shaped.
